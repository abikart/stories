# Fern and the Silent Seed Bells

In Lanternleaf Forest, morning began with seed bells.

Their bright chime woke every window and warm oven.

But one rain-washed morning, the bells were silent.

Fern found their ribbons twisted around the bell tree.

She loosened the low knots while Pipkin slipped the middle ones free.

Still, the highest ribbon hugged a thorny branch.

“I cannot reach it.”

“I can, if you show me a path through the thorns.”

Fern traced a silver path through the leaves.

At the top, Pipkin reached the knotted ribbon.

One careful tug opened the knot.

The breeze rushed in.

The seed bells bobbed and chimed—one, two, three!

All at once, the forest morning opened around them.

“You reached what I could not.”

“You saw the way I could not.”

Above them, the bell tree played the morning they had made together.

## Story intent

- Audience: ages 5–7.
- Target performance: about 78 seconds, including expressive gaps and the
  interaction-owned silent beat.
- Emotional shape: familiar morning ritual → surprising hush → shared effort →
  one unreachable problem → complementary help → communal release.
- Theme: friendship makes different abilities useful without explaining a moral.
- Hero interaction: after Fern draws the path, the child guides Pipkin through
  the thorny canopy to the high knot. Watch and film perform the same path
  canonically during the authored pause.
